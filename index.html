North Carolina Infant Mortality: Autocorrelation Study

A rigorous statistical analysis of infant mortality patterns across North Carolina. This project moves beyond simple heatmaps to prove statistical significance using global and local spatial autocorrelation metrics.

  • Methodology: Comparative analysis using Global Moran’s I and Getis-Ord G* to identify validated high-rate clusters (Hotspots) in the NE and SE regions.
  • Spatial Insights: Differentiated between "count" vs. "rate" patterns to identify socioeconomic outliers and statistically significant "Low-High" coastal anomalies.
  • Validation: Rejecting null hypotheses via Z-scores and P-value assessment to ensure data-driven public health conclusions.

Lincoln City: Spatio-Temporal Crime Pattern Analysis

A multi-methodological study of auto-theft patterns in Lincoln City, Oregon. This project utilizes hierarchical clustering and moving averages to provide actionable intelligence for law enforcement resource allocation.

  • Clustering: Applied Nearest Neighbor Hierarchical (NNH) spatial clustering across multiple radii to identify 1st and 2nd order crime hotspots.
  • Temporal Modeling: Developed a Spatio-Temporal Moving Average (STMA) to track the geographic "drift" of incidents over time.
  • Comparison: Evaluated NNA vs. NNH to prove why hierarchical structures provide superior operational insights over simple distribution measures.
